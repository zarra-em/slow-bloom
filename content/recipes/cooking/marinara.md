"measure to taste": i've always loved how cooking teaches you to trust yourself. 

**warning**: the reason this is a marinara and not a tomato sauce is because of its simplicity: the ingredient profile is minimalist, and it is a quick cook. that being said, there is a lot of room for creativity.

**music pairing**: louis prima!!
**podcast pairing**: the debaters

**INGREDIENTS**
=
**COOKING POT**
- **3 shallots** (chop them up like a madman; could be substituted with an onion)
- **2-3 garlic** (dice them up)
- **white wine**
- **olive oil** (a generous amount)
- **1 can of fire-roasted tomatoes**
- **1 can of regular diced tomatoes**
- **sprig of rosemary
- **sprig of thyme**
- **1 or 2 bay leaves**
- **cinnamon** (to taste)
- **oregano** (to taste)
- **salt** (to taste)
- **pepper** (to taste)
- **regular, smoked, or sweet paprika** (to taste)

**STEPS**
=
1. chop up the shallots and garlic (mise en place saves lives).
2. add a generous amount of olive oil to a cooking pot. turn the stove on to medium-high.
3. add the shallots to the heated oil, cooking until they become soft, translucent, and slightly browned.
	- note: if you want to make the sauce more flavourful, you could add a tablespoon of butter at this stage. you could also caramelize the shallots (consistently mix them and make sure they don't burn).
4. once the shallots are cooked, turn down the heat and add in the garlic (garlic can burn pretty easily, so only cook until golden brown and fragrant).
5. deglaze the pot by adding a bit of white wine (dry white wine is best).
6. add in the 2 cans of tomatoes, followed by the rosemary, thyme, bay leaves, cinnamon, oregano, salt, pepper, and paprika (taste the sauce so you know what ingredients need to be added).
