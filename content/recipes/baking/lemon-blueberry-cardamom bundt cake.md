*this* is probably my favourite recipe. the cardamom has a hypnotizing effect on the masses.

**warning**: this recipe requires a lot of patience (but it's totally worth it).

**tips**: mise en place saves lives. cardamom pods offer more flavour than powdered cardamom. if you forget to take your eggs out ahead of time, put them in a bowl of lukewarm water as a tempering technique. sift the flour and sugar (or just sift all the dry ingredients to ensure there are no clumps). make the buttermilk before starting on anything else so it warms to room temp (if you forget to do this, it's not the end of the world). you can also use the batter to make muffins or a loaf (just make sure you don't overfill the loaf pan).

**music pairing**: soul or funk. 
**podcast pairing**: louder than a riot or philosophize this.

**INGREDIENTS**
=
**BOWL 1: BIG BOWL**
- **360 g all-purpose flour**
- **300 g granulated sugar**
- **1 tbsp cornstarch**
- **2 tsp baking *powder***
- **1/2 tsp baking *soda***
- **1 tsp cinnamon**
- **2 tsp cardamom** (take the cardamom seeds out of the pods, put them in a mortar and pestle, grind until fine)
- **2 zested lemons**
- **pinch of salt**

**SAUCEPAN/POT**
- **113 g brown butter** (measure the butter into a pot, turn the stove on to medium-high, melt until golden brown, cool to room temp, add to bowl 1)

**LIQUID MEASURING CUP: 1 CUP**
- **1/3 cup vegetable oil** (i've never tried it, but you could probably experiment by using different variations of oil: e.g., avocado oil, coconut oil, canola oil)
- **1/6 cup olive oil**

**LIQUID MEASURING CUP: 2 CUP**
- **3/4 cup buttermilk** (i use 2% milk and lemon juice, but you can use any type of milk and substitute lemon juice for vinegar)
- **4 large eggs** (i always take out the chalaza—kəˈleɪzə/kuhˈ-lay-zuh)
- **1 tbsp rosewater or orange blossom water or both** (totally optional since these might be niche ingredients)
- **1.5 juiced lemons**
- **vanilla extract** (pour until fragrant or measure 1–2 tsp if you're a stickler)
- **almond extract** (totally optional. again, pour until fragrant or measure 1/2–1 tsp if you're a stickler)

**BOWL 2: MEDIUM BOWL**
- **340 g blueberries** (i always use frozen. DO NOT THAW. you could probably use fresh if you wanted)
- **15 g all-purpose flour** (coating the blueberries in flour ensures that they don't sink to the bottom of the cake)

**STEPS**
=
1. bowl 1: add and whisk together the respective ingredients for bowl 1. set aside.
	- notes: make sure to sift the flour and sugar to break up clumps. use a mortar and pestle to grind cardamom seeds.
2. measure the butter into a pot, turn the stove on to medium-high, melt until golden brown, cool to room temp. set aside.
	- tip: keep the butter in the pot until it is time to combine it with other ingredients.
3. liquid measuring cup (size: **1 cup**): add the respective ingredients into the 1 cup liquid measuring cup. set aside.
	- notes: measure the vegetable oil to the 1/3 line and then add in the olive oil until the mixture reaches the 1/2 line (simple math: 1/3 + 1/6 = 1/2).
4. liquid measuring cup (size: **2 cups**): add the respective ingredients into the 2 cups liquid measuring cup. set aside.
	- make the buttermilk using your choice of milk and 1 cap of vinegar OR 1 tbsp of lemon juice. stir with a spoon and let sit for 5 minutes. 
	- in a separate bowl, crack in the eggs and whisk for 20 seconds. add the eggs into the 2 cups liquid measuring cup. add in rosewater, lemon juice, vanilla extract, and almond extract. whisk to combine.
5. set the oven to 325 °C. grease a 10-inch bundt pan.
	- note: i usually grease the pan with butter, but you can use oil or cooking spray. i also like to dust the pan with flour.
6. for the following steps, make sure to use an electric mixer on low speed. drizzle and combine the brown butter and the oils (from the 1 cup liquid measuring cup) into bowl 1. the flour may clump, but try your best to create a sandy-like consistency. 
7. drizzle and combine the contents of the 2 cups liquid measuring cup (buttermilk mixture) into bowl 1. scrape down the edges with a spatula. the mixture may have clumps, but those will dissolve when baked. set aside.
8. in a separate bowl, toss the blueberries in all-purpose flour.
9. fold in the flour-coated blueberries into bowl 1 (at this point, all the ingredients should be in bowl 1)
10. fill the bundt pan 2/3 of the way full and pop it in the oven. you will probably have extra batter that you can use to make muffins. 
	- digression: if you're using the batter to make muffins, wait to put the muffins in the oven until after the cake is done. in the meantime, you can cover the batter and refrigerate it. when you're ready, bake @ 350 °C for about 10–12 minutes (or until golden brown and fully baked—use a toothpick). 
11. bake the cake @ 325 °C for about 8 minutes and then turn up the oven to 350 °C for about 22–30 minutes (or until golden brown, slightly cracked, and passes the toothpick test).
	- note: you could also use a thermometer—make sure the internal temperature is at least 200 °F (93 °C) and does not exceed 210 °F (98 °C).
12. take the cake out of the oven and let it cool down to room temperature before extracting it from the pan (make sure to say 1 apostle's creed, 3 hail marys, 2 our fathers, and a glory be before taking it out to make sure it doesn't stick). if you want, you can also make a glaze or icing for it.
	- i usually make a glaze and drizzle it on top: 1 cup icing sugar, 1 tbsp lemon juice, and 1-2 tbsp of milk (adjust measurements based on desired consistency)
13. enjoy :)
